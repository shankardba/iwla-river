About

About this record

An independent analysis of the Izaak Walton League's Save Our Streams data, for the people who run monitoring programs and the scientists who use the results. It joins the volunteer record with public datasets on land, water quality, flow, permits and professional surveys, tests each question with a stated method, and cites every source. It is not an official IWLA product.

What was joined

Every volunteer site is first placed in its HUC12 watershed and on its stream segment. That spine is what lets the other datasets attach: some by segment, most by watershed, some by watershed and date, and the paired comparisons by distance and date. Hidden kit and tap sites join by watershed only.

How each finding is made

Each question gets the test that fits it, chosen before the result is known, and every result reports its effect size, its uncertainty and its q-value. Results that come out null are kept and shown.

Safeguards

  • Repeat visits. Sites are surveyed many times. Intervals come from a cluster bootstrap that resamples whole sites or watersheds, and regressions use cluster-robust errors, so one busy site cannot pass for many.
  • Many tests. Trends are tested at hundreds of sites and thousands of watersheds. Each family of tests is corrected with Benjamini–Hochberg q-values, and only those under 0.05 are called a trend.
  • Models scored fairly. The driver models are scored on sites they never saw (five repeats of five-fold cross-validation grouped by site), and their range across repeats is reported.
  • Policy effects. Before-and-after comparisons near restoration projects are run again on a placebo date. When the placebo shift is as large as the real one, as it is here, the finding says no benefit is detectable.
  • Volunteer data checked against professionals. Volunteer chloride is compared with agency lab results, and volunteer bug scores with professional surveys at the same places. See the chloride check and volunteers vs professionals.

Limits

  • Volunteer sites are chosen by volunteers, not at random, so national figures describe the monitored streams, not all streams. Monitoring gaps shows where coverage is thin.
  • A watershed join matches the same HUC12, not always the same reach; associations at watershed level do not prove what happens at a single site.
  • Associations are not causes. Where a finding says "linked with", that is all it claims.
  • Test-strip chemistry is less precise than lab chemistry; how much less is measured in the chloride check, not assumed.

Reproduce it

The code is public at github.com/shankardba/iwla-river. Every finding page lists its sources, and the site build stops if a finding cites a source that is not in References. Sources and methods describes the cleaning rules and the score.

Privacy

Personal details are removed when the data is downloaded and never shown. Many salt and nitrate kits test home taps, so those sites carry no names and their locations are rounded to about 1 km.

Using Explore

Explore maps every sample. Browse by watershed, from region down to subwatershed, or by state, where each state's subbasins are drawn inside it. Each watershed shows its own cited findings; press / to search. Findings collects the national results, and Case study: Great Seneca Creek follows one creek in depth.
