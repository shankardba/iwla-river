Volunteer chloride tracks agency lab chloride

Good news Where a state or federal agency measured chloride in the same small watershed within a week, volunteer SOS chemistry readings agree closely (correlation 0.70; 86% within a factor of two); Salt Watch strips agree more loosely (0.47).

0.70SOS chemistry vs agency lab (log scale)
0.47Salt Watch strip vs agency lab
8,732matched readings

Volunteer chloride can only inform decisions if it measures what a laboratory would. Each volunteer reading was paired with the median of agency laboratory chloride results reported to the Water Quality Portal from the same 12-digit watershed (HUC12, about 100 km²) within seven days. Readings of exactly 0 and the one impossible value were left out.

Correlation of log10 chloride; interval from 2,000 bootstrap resamples of whole watersheds.

The SOS chemistry kit's median ratio to the agency value is 1.00: no overall bias. Salt Watch strips read in coarse colour steps, so they scatter more. In winter they read higher than the agency results (97 vs 80 mg/L median, 2,633 pairs, Wilcoxon signed-rank p < 0.001), likely because volunteers test after storms and near roads while agencies sample on fixed schedules at fixed stations.

A watershed-and-week match is looser than a same-spot, same-hour match, so these correlations understate how well the kits measure.

How this was worked out

Pairs: chloride_pairs in the joined dataset (Clean Water Hub snapshot of 2026-10-27; joined public data as of 2026-10-28). Pearson correlation of log10 values; Spearman rank correlation 0.78 (SOS) and 0.47 (Salt Watch); cluster bootstrap by watershed; Wilcoxon signed-rank test on winter log ratios. Code: iwla_river/context.py.
