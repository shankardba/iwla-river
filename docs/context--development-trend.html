Paving keeps spreading, fastest where volunteers work

Watch The median watershed with an IWLA bug site went from 1.3% to 2.0% paved between 1985 and 2025, gaining more than other watersheds nearby (+0.6 vs +0.35 points); within a watershed, bug scores have not yet tracked that growth.

1.3% → 2.0%median paved, IWLA watersheds, 1985–2025
12%of IWLA watersheds gained 5+ points of paving
p = 0.27score change as a watershed paves

Volunteers monitor where people live, so their watersheds are more built up to begin with and are being built up faster. 12% of them gained five or more percentage points of paving since 1985, and 5% gained ten or more.

Between watersheds, more paving goes with lower bug scores (see the salt and development page). Within a watershed over time, the change is too small and slow to show yet: comparing each watershed with itself across years (142 watersheds, 1,088 watershed-years), spring scores move +0.016 standard deviations per point of new paving (p = 0.27). Most of these watersheds gained under one point in the years volunteers surveyed them.

How this was worked out

Annual NLCD fractional impervious, pixels averaged inside each HUC12 outline. Within-watershed test: spring scores (SOS and Virginia forms on one standardised scale) and paving with watershed and year means removed, least squares with standard errors clustered by watershed; watersheds with three or more surveyed years. Clean Water Hub snapshot of 2026-10-27; joined public data as of 2026-10-28. Code: iwla_river/context.py.
