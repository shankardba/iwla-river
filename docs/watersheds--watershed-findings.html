How the findings for each watershed are worked out

Context Pick any watershed on the Explore map to see what the data show there: 28,015 units have findings of their own, each a plain sentence with its numbers and numbered sources.

28,015watersheds with findings
271trend tests
13driver models
FindingHowShown when
Trend in pollution-sensitive animals and in the survey scoreEach survey relative to its own site's usual level; seasonal Kendall test on season-year means, Sen slope; false discovery rate held at 5% across all units of the same size (Benjamini–Hochberg)8+ years, 3+ sites, 30+ surveys
Chloride rising or fallingSeasonal Kendall test on log chloride at each site; Benjamini–Hochberg across all 396 sites testeda site with 8+ readings over 4+ years
Paved and roofed area, 1985 to 2025Annual NLCD fractional impervious cover averaged over each small watershed (HUC12), then area-weighted over the unitalways, within IWLA's river basins
Impairment listings and dischargersEPA ATTAINS (January 2025 snapshot): waters impaired for aquatic life or for salt; EPA ECHO: permit holders in significant noncompliancealways, within IWLA's river basins
Volunteer coverageSmall watersheds with a bug site surveyed since 2021; high need = 5+ pressure flags (see In context: monitoring gaps)always, within IWLA's river basins
What predicts the sensitive shareGradient-boosted trees (LightGBM) on watershed traits from EPA StreamCat plus time of year and survey form; five repeats of five folds grouped by site, so no site is in both training and test; TreeSHAP on the held-out surveys; direction from the rank correlation of each trait with its contributionregions down to subbasins with 40+ biological sites

A finding for a large unit can hide opposite changes in its parts, and a trend in a small unit rests on few sites, so read each with its counts. Units with too little data show none. Sites are compared with themselves, so a unit gaining or losing sites does not create a trend by itself.

Run on the M4 mini by iwla_river/watershed_findings.py from the Clean Water Hub snapshot of 2026-10-27 and the joined public data of 2026-10-28; 2 sensitive-share trends and 10 score trends pass the 5% false discovery rate.

How this was worked out

Code: iwla_river/watershed_findings.py. Results: iwla_river/web/wsf/<region>.json, one sentence per finding with the keys of its sources.
