Paved land, more than winter salt, tracks low bug scores

Context Across 140 watersheds, saltier winters go with lower spring bug scores, but that link disappears once paved area is accounted for; paving remains a strong predictor (p < 0.001).

0.66correlation of paving with winter salt
-0.5SD of score per 10 points of paving
401watershed-years

Road salt and pavement come together: the more a watershed is paved, the more salt goes on it. To separate them, each watershed's winter salt was measured from agency laboratory chloride, USGS gauge conductance and volunteer chloride (each on a log scale, standardised and averaged), and set against the next spring's bug scores and the watershed's paved share from the annual national land cover maps.

ModelChange in spring score (SD)Test
Winter salt alone-0.37 per SD of saltp < 0.001
Paving, with salt in the model-0.050 per point of paving (-0.5 per 10 points)p < 0.001
Salt, with paving in the model+0.03 per SD of saltp = 0.71
Saltier winter than usual, same watershed-0.09 per SD (SE 0.08)p = 0.25

Scores standardised within each survey form (SOS, Virginia SOS) so both can be combined.

Paving and salt are strongly linked (r = 0.66), so on their own both look harmful. With both in the model, paving keeps its effect and salt adds nothing. Within the same watershed, a winter saltier than usual is followed by a slightly lower spring score, but not reliably (78 watersheds with two or more years, p = 0.25).

This does not clear road salt. Salt is one of the ways paving harms streams, and spring bug surveys may miss short winter spikes. It does mean the volunteer data cannot yet show a salt effect separate from development.

How this was worked out

Between watersheds: least squares on watershed means with heteroskedasticity-robust (HC1) standard errors. Within: watershed and year effects removed by two-way demeaning, standard errors clustered by watershed. Paving interpolated between the 5-yearly Annual NLCD maps. Clean Water Hub snapshot of 2026-10-27; joined public data as of 2026-10-28. Code: iwla_river/context.py.
