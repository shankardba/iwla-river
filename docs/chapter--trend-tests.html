Trend tests

Concern At Site 1 the share of pollution-sensitive groups is declining (p = 0.042) while the total score shows no clear trend.

A seasonal Kendall test compares each survey only with surveys from the same season in other years, so the spring-summer gap cannot pose as a trend. One value per season and year is used, and a trend is reported only with at least 10 same-season comparisons.

Mayfly, stonefly and caddisfly groups at Site 1 are also declining (p = 0.092). Site 2 has fewer surveys and shows no clear trend. No sudden shift stands out at either site: the strongest candidate at Site 1, around 2021, would arise by chance 39% of the time.

Results with p between 0.05 and 0.1 are a signal to watch rather than proof.

How this was worked out

Seasonal Kendall test (Hirsch, Slack and Smith 1982) on season-year medians; seasonal Sen slope; turning points by a permutation test of the strongest mean shift (999 shuffles).
