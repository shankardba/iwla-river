Chloride is rising at more sites than it is falling

Watch Of 396 sites with at least eight chloride readings over four or more years, 69 show a rising trend and 12 a falling one after correcting for testing many sites at once.

69sites rising
12sites falling
+6%a year, median at rising sites

Freshwater across the northern United States has been getting saltier for decades, mainly from road salt, and chloride above 230 mg/L harms stream animals. Among the volunteer sites with long enough records, rising sites outnumber falling ones 69 to 12; at the rising sites chloride climbs by a median of 6% a year.

Across the whole network the picture is not significant (seasonal Kendall on monthly medians of each reading relative to its own site: -1% a decade, p = 0.10). That network figure mixes thousands of short-lived Salt Watch sites that join and leave, so the site-by-site count is the better measure of change.

Data quality: 2,138 chloride readings are exactly 0, which a kit cannot read in a salted stream (most likely blanks or 'below detection'), and one reading is 534,545 mg/L, about 28 times seawater. All of these were left out.

How this was worked out

39,690 readings at 3,050 sites, 2002-05 to 2026-10 (Clean Water Hub snapshot of 2026-10-27; joined public data as of 2026-10-28). Per site: seasonal Kendall test on log10 chloride with seasons as quarters, Sen slope; Benjamini–Hochberg q < 0.05. Network: monthly median of each reading minus its site's median, months with 3+ readings. Code: iwla_river/context.py.
