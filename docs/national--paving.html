Paved watersheds lose sensitive animals

Context Nationally, sensitive animals fall from 40% of groups found in near-natural watersheds to 22% where a quarter or more is paved.

40%sensitive share where under 2% is paved
22%where a quarter or more is paved
7,326surveys

Stream ecologists call this the urban stream syndrome: the more of a watershed is paved or roofed, the faster rain runs off it, carrying heat, salt and sediment, and the fewer pollution-sensitive animals live downstream.

The steepest losses come early: sensitive animals thin out well before a watershed is a quarter paved. Paving explains low scores better than winter road salt does once both are measured, and each watershed's own paving since 1985 is shown when you pick it on the Explore map.
