Stream restoration: no gain beyond what a placebo shows

Context Virginia SOS scores near completed stream restorations rise +0.40 points more than at comparison sites, but a fake completion date five years earlier shows a rise just as large (+0.50).

+0.40gain vs comparison sites
+0.50same test with a fake date
5.3 vs 9.2starting scores, restored vs comparison
TestProjectsChange near projectChange at comparison sitesDifference
At completion58+0.37-0.04+0.40 (95% CI 0.18 to 0.61)
Placebo: 5 years earlier38+0.49-0.01+0.50 (95% CI 0.29 to 0.72)

Mean Virginia SOS score 1–5 years after minus 1–5 years before; sites within 5 km of a project; comparison sites in the same state with no project of any kind within 5 km.

Sites near restorations start far lower (5.3 vs 9.2 on a 0–12 scale), and low scores tend to drift back up whatever happens. The placebo test, with each project's date moved five years earlier, shows that drift: the gain appears before any work was done.

So the volunteer data cannot show a restoration benefit. That is not proof restorations fail. Many aim at channel stability or nutrient credits rather than stream animals, and recovery can take longer than five years.

How this was worked out

Completed stream restoration projects 1995–2022 with a place and year; difference in differences with comparison sites; 95% intervals from 2,000 bootstrap resamples of projects. Clean Water Hub snapshot of 2026-10-27; joined public data as of 2026-10-28. Code: iwla_river/context.py.
