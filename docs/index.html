Great Seneca Creek Record

46 stream surveys at two sites between 2005 and 2026, read against 83,297 volunteer surveys nationwide. The creek's overall score has held steady, but its share of pollution-sensitive animals appears to be slipping below what its setting predicts.

Key findings

Great Seneca CreekWatch

Compared with what its setting predicts

Site 1 finds fewer pollution-sensitive animals than its setting predicts, and the gap appears to be widening (p = 0.055, about 11 percentage points a decade).

 Great Seneca CreekSteady

The score has held steady

Scores held near 16.5 before 2013 and 16.6 since; the overall score shows no trend.

 Great Seneca CreekWatch

The mix of animals is shifting

Between 2005–2009 and 2020–2024, damselflies and common net-spinning caddisflies became rarer while dobsonflies and aquatic worms became regulars.

Road salt reaches the creek every winter

Streams across the Potomac are mostly improving

11 of 31 long-record sites within 30 km show improving scores, the opposite of Site 1's direction.

Paved watersheds lose sensitive animals

Nationally, sensitive animals fall from 40% of groups found in near-natural watersheds to 22% where a quarter or more is paved.

How volunteers sample

Save Our Streams volunteers work in riffles, the fast, shallow, stony stretches of a stream. They stir the stones upstream of a net and count the animals that wash in.
