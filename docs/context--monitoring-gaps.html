Most high-need watersheds have no volunteer monitor

Concern Of 1,093 watersheds with five or more pressures, 1,039 have no active volunteer bug site; 135 of those are within 25 km of one.

278of 22,697 watersheds with an active bug site
1,039high-need watersheds unmonitored
135of them within 25 km of a volunteer

Every small watershed (HUC12) in the river basins where IWLA volunteers work was scored for need: one point each for any impaired water, a salt impairment, a major discharge permit, a discharger in significant noncompliance, 10% or more paved, 5 or more points of new paving since 1985 and coal or hard-rock mine features, and two for impaired biology.

Monitored high-need watersheds are wealthier than unmonitored ones (median household income $108,189 vs $92,081, p = 0.02). Within 25 km of an active volunteer the gap shrinks ($108,189 vs $96,379, p = 0.08), so it mostly follows where volunteers live.

How this was worked out

22,697 HUC12s from the USGS Watershed Boundary Dataset; pressures from EPA ATTAINS, EPA ECHO, Annual NLCD and USGS USMIN; income from ACS 2020–2024 tracts. Mann–Whitney U tests. Clean Water Hub snapshot of 2026-10-27; joined public data as of 2026-10-28. Code: iwla_river/context.py.
