Volunteers and professionals mostly agree, but volunteers rate generously

Context Where a Virginia SOS survey sits within 1 km of a Maryland state survey, the two agree on condition 67% of the time (weighted kappa 0.50), as well as Maryland's own trained volunteers do; volunteers more often call a stream healthier than the professionals.

Condition classes Good, Fair, Poor from each program's own thresholds. Kappa: 0 = chance agreement, 1 = perfect.

Professional and volunteer surveys rarely visit the same spot on the same day, so most pairs are matched by watershed and year, which adds disagreement that has nothing to do with skill. The closest matches (within 1 km) are few: 18 pairs at 7 sites. They agree as well as Maryland's trained Stream Waders volunteers do with the state's professionals, which is the fairest benchmark.

The Virginia SOS method, built to track professional assessments, agrees far better than the original SOS count of animal groups, which barely tracks the professional surveys. When volunteers and professionals differ, volunteers usually rate the stream higher, as earlier studies of volunteer biomonitoring found.

How this was worked out

Pairs from pro_pairs and md_volunteer_pairs in the joined dataset (Clean Water Hub snapshot of 2026-10-27; joined public data as of 2026-10-28). Spearman rank correlation of scores; Cohen's weighted kappa (linear weights) on condition classes. Code: iwla_river/context.py.
